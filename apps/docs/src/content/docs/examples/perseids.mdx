Every August, Earth crosses the dust trail of comet 109P/Swift–Tuttle, and the grains burn up as
the Perseid meteor shower. All of its meteors appear to fly away from one point in the sky, the
radiant, in the constellation Perseus. That makes a shower one of the few subjects where a radial
Burst is not a metaphor but the real geometry.

Scroll through one night, from dusk to dawn. It reads best on its own page.

## What motly does in it

- **Hundreds of Elements on canvas.** About 560 twinkling stars, and up to about 100 meteor
  streaks at once, are painted on canvas with `renderer: 'canvas'`.
- **Specs built from data.** Each hour's meteors come from the rate the model gives for that hour:
  one streak per meteor, split into near, mid and far Bursts.
- **An Anchor that moves.** The radiant climbs as you scroll. Each hour's Bursts start from wherever
  it has climbed to, because an Anchor's position is read when its burst starts.
- **Seeds.** Each hour, and each replay within it, has its own Seed, so an hour always rains the
  same meteors. The fireball at 02:30 is scrubbed with the scroll, and scrolling back runs it back
  exactly.
- **Swirl and Bursts of Bursts.** The fireball breaks into fragments that curl as they fall, and the
  star field is three levels of Bursts.
- **Reduced motion.** A reader who prefers it sees each hour's Resting frame instead of the rain. The
  tiles at the end are those frames, drawn by `@motly/core` alone from the same Specs and Seeds.

## Streaks that point outward

A `line` runs from 12 o'clock to 6, whichever way it is thrown. `orient: true` on each hour's Burst
turns every streak along its ray, so the meteors point away from the radiant, as real ones do.

The fireball is a Burst of one Child, aimed with `angle: -60`: up and to the left of the radiant,
with `orient` laying the streak along the throw. See
[aiming a Burst](../../reference/burst/#aiming-angle-and-spread).

`rand()` on a Burst's `radius` resolves once per Burst, not per Child: it is the Emitter's
distance. So each hour is three Bursts, near, mid and far, with different counts, whose rays do not
line up.

## The model

The rates are a model, not a night's counts. The International Meteor Organization gives the
Perseids a zenithal hourly rate (ZHR) of 100 at their peak, with the radiant at right ascension 48°
and declination +58°. The ZHR is what an ideal observer would see with the radiant overhead, so the
rate the story uses is ZHR × sin(radiant altitude), for a dark site at 50° N on the peak night, in
local solar time. It leaves out twilight and the Moon, which both cut real counts.

| Time | Radiant altitude | Meteors an hour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 21:00 | 25° | 42 |
| 22:00 | 30° | 50 |
| 23:00 | 36° | 58 |
| 00:00 | 42° | 67 |
| 01:00 | 49° | 76 |
| 02:00 | 57° | 84 |
| 03:00 | 65° | 91 |
| 04:00 | 73° | 95 |

Source: the IMO 2024 Meteor Shower Calendar, Perseids (007 PER).
